Summer garden planner

Five questions, then a planting plan you can print and follow: what to buy, when to sow it, and where each thing goes in the bed.

Where are you growing?

Everything downstream is dates, and dates come from frost.

Your USDA zone

Don't know it? Search "hardiness zone" and your zip code.

A zone is a winter number. It describes how cold your coldest night gets, which decides what survives the winter — and everything in a summer garden is planted and finished inside one year. What actually matters is your last spring frost, so that is what the zone is converted into below. It is an average: a hilltop and the valley under it can differ by two or three weeks. If you know your real date, set it.

How much direct sun does the spot get?

Midsummer, at its best. Count only hours of unshaded sun.

What do you actually eat?

Pick what you'd genuinely be pleased to have a lot of. Nothing else earns its space.

How much do you want?

This sets how much of the bed each choice gets.

How big is the bed?

One rectangle. If you have several, plan the biggest first — you can run this again.

Width runs left to right on the plan, depth runs away from you. Keep the depth to about 4 ft if it is a raised bed — that is as far as most people can reach in from an edge. The plan puts the tallest plants along the top edge — set the bed out so that top edge points north and nothing shades anything behind it.
